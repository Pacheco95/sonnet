# ADR-0038: A character controller on Jolt's CharacterVirtual, its frame order and animation weights

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Amends:** [ADR-0009](0009-physics-and-scripting.md), [ADR-0022](0022-gameplay-events-and-script-properties.md) and [ADR-0023](0023-animation-blending-morph-targets-and-particles.md)
- **Date:** 2026-11-07

## Context

The Third-Person Character release (`apps/samples/character`, [#488](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/488) and the tasks it names) needs a walking, jumping character whose capsule is simulated by physics, whose model is posed by blended locomotion clips with no visible foot sliding, and whose camera follows without jitter. Three modules meet there: `physics` moves the capsule, `world` animates the model and propagates transforms, `scripting` turns input into commands and places the camera. This ADR settles the contracts they implement: T2 ([#489](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/489)) in `physics`, T4 ([#491](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/491)) in `world`, T6 ([#493](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/493)) in `scripting`.

What the code does today:

- `physics` knows static, kinematic and dynamic bodies only (`JoltPhysicsWorld.cpp`). `PhysicsStep` in `FixedUpdate` destroys stale bodies, creates missing ones, pushes poses in (static bodies teleported, kinematic ones `MoveKinematic`, a dynamic body teleported when its `Transform` differs from what physics last wrote), calls `PhysicsSystem::Update`, turns the contact records into events, and writes the dynamic bodies' poses out. `PhysicsInterpolate` in `PostUpdate` writes each dynamic body's pose between its last two steps by `World::fixedAlpha`. Bodies are mapped to entities by Jolt body id (`m_bodyInfo`), which the contact listener reads.
- `scripting` declares `ScriptFixedUpdate` in `FixedUpdate` after `PhysicsStep`, so contact events and `fixedUpdate` follow the step and anything a script asks for there reaches the next step, and `ScriptUpdate` in `Update`. There is no `lateUpdate`.
- `AnimationPlayback` runs in `Update`, declared before `ScriptUpdate` because the animation system is constructed first. The primary clip's weight is the crossfade envelope alone, held in the system's private `PlayState`: it is 1 except while a crossfade ramps it up, and an author cannot lower it. Every layer with a positive weight and a channel for a target contributes to the weight-normalised blend; a crossfade pushes the outgoing clip into `Animator.layers` at its time with a negative `fadeRate`. Nothing synchronises two clips' phases.
- `Game::update` and `Editor::update` read `world::sceneCamera` before `world.progress(dt)`, so the drawn camera is the one the previous frame's transform system placed, one frame behind everything the frame moves. With a camera that follows a moving character, that is a one-frame lag the eye sees as judder.
- The scripting API already has `Entity:worldPosition()`, current before the transform system runs, and `physics.raycast(origin, direction, maxDistance, ignore)`; sphere casts (`sphereCast(origin, radius, direction, maxDistance, ignore)`) are being added in [#507](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/507).

What the pinned Jolt (5.6.0) does, established by `modules/physics/tests/CharacterFeasibilityTests.cpp` (`physics_tests "[character]"`, eleven cases, well under a second):

- `CharacterVirtual::ExtendedUpdate` does not integrate gravity. A character two metres above a floor, updated for a second with gravity passed in and zero velocity, stays at 2 m and `InAir`; the gravity argument only presses on what the character stands on. The wrapper owns the velocity.
- The capsule built with its bottom at the character's origin rests with the origin on the floor to within a millimetre and stays there. Against a wall the capsule stops `mCharacterPadding` (2 cm) short.
- `WalkStairs`, which `ExtendedUpdate` calls only while the character is pushed into a contact too steep to walk, is the step height: with a 0.3 m step-up a 0.25 m step is climbed and a 0.45 m one blocks, and with the step-up at zero the 0.25 m step blocks too.
- With a 50° maximum slope a 30° ramp is walked up and a 60° one is not.
- `StickToFloor`, called by `ExtendedUpdate` when the character goes from supported to unsupported without moving up, is the ground snap. Running down a 40° ramp at 5 m/s is 23 airborne steps without it and none with 0.3 m; walking off a 0.6 m ledge with 0.3 m is still a fall of about 16 steps that lands on the floor.
- Adding gravity to the velocity while the character stands on walkable ground makes it creep down a 30° slope (16 cm in two seconds); not adding it while standing gives no creep at all, and a character on a 60° slope still slides to the bottom.
- A jump of 5 m/s leaves walkable ground on the very step that consumes it, peaks near v²/2g and lands about a second later.
- Moved in the order "kinematic `MoveKinematic`, then the character, then `PhysicsSystem::Update`", a character standing on a platform moving sideways at 1 m/s moves 1 m in a second, exactly with it, without any intent of its own; on a lift rising at 2 m/s it stays walkable at the lift's height, and when the lift stops abruptly it stays walkable on it rather than being thrown up. Jumping off the sideways platform keeps 1 m/s of horizontal velocity in the air after the platform stops.
- With the default 100 N strength the character pushes a 20 kg crate and is stopped by a 1000 kg one. The pushes are `AddImpulse` calls made inside `ExtendedUpdate` through the no-lock body interface, so the character must never update while `PhysicsSystem::Update` runs.
- With an inner body (`CharacterVirtualSettings::mInnerBodyShape`), a ray cast down onto the character hits that kinematic body, `IgnoreSingleBodyFilter` on it lets the ray through, and the character's own sweeps never collide with it. Without one, a `CharacterVirtual` is invisible to every query.
- A static sensor sees the inner body enter and leave. Jolt moves the inner body with `SetPositionAndRotation` without activating it, and a static sensor sees only active bodies; the body stayed awake through ten seconds of standing in the probe, but nothing in Jolt promises it, so the test turns sleeping off on it (`Body::SetAllowSleeping(false)`). The character's own listener reports the same sensor as added and removed.

## Decision

### The component

`CharacterController` is a reflected component of `physics`, registered with the others by `registerComponents`:

| Field | Unit | Default | Valid |
|---|---|---|---|
| `radius` | m | 0.3 | > 0 |
| `halfHeight` | m, of the cylinder | 0.6 (a 1.8 m capsule) | ≥ 0 |
| `offset` | m, entity space | (0, 0, 0) | finite |
| `maxSlope` | radians (flecs `Radians`, shown in degrees) | 50° | > 0 and < 90° |
| `stepHeight` | m | 0.3 | ≥ 0 and ≤ `halfHeight + radius` |
| `groundSnap` | m | 0.3 | ≥ 0 and ≤ `halfHeight + radius` |
| `mass` | kg | 70 | > 0 |
| `pushStrength` | N | 100 | ≥ 0 |

- **Origin.** The entity's origin is the character's feet: the capsule stands upright along world +Y with its bottom at the origin plus `offset` (Jolt's shape is a `RotatedTranslatedShape` lifting the capsule by `halfHeight + radius`), so a model child placed at the origin stands on the ground. The capsule is never scaled. Contacts below the centre of the lower half-sphere support the character (`mSupportingVolume = Plane(up, -radius)`); higher ones only collide.
- **Validation.** A value outside its range is a configuration error. The controller is then rejected: it gets no character, every command on it returns false and `character` returns nothing, and the reason is logged once, until one of its physics components changes. Values are not clamped, so what the inspector shows is what was simulated.
- **No mixing with bodies.** An entity with a `CharacterController` must not have a `RigidBody`, a collider or `Trigger`, must not have a `CharacterController` or a `RigidBody` above it, and no enabled collider may be in its subtree (a collider there would be a static body of its own, moving with the character and blocking it). Any of these rejects the controller as above, on creation and whenever the step finds the combination: the step already re-checks bodies for added, removed or moved colliders, prefab instantiation and reparenting included, and checks controllers the same way. The other entities keep their bodies.
- **Parents and scale.** The controller should be a root. Under a parent it is simulated in world space and its local `Transform` is written relative to the parent, as a dynamic body's is; a parent that moves does not carry it, since riding something is what support is for. Its world rotation must be a yaw about +Y and its world scale 1 (to 1e-3); otherwise it is rejected. Visual facing is a child's rotation, not the controller's.

### Commands and state

`IPhysicsWorld` gains, and Lua's `physics` table mirrors with the same names:

| C++ | Lua | Contract |
|---|---|---|
| `bool setCharacterMove(flecs::entity, glm::vec3 horizontalVelocity)` | `physics.setCharacterMove(entity, v)` | World-space intent in m/s, held until changed. Its `y` must be 0 (to 1e-4) and every component finite; otherwise it is rejected: C++ returns false and keeps the previous intent, Lua raises an error naming the value. No speed limit: the script decides speeds. |
| `bool jumpCharacter(flecs::entity, float verticalSpeed)` | `physics.jumpCharacter(entity, speed)` | One-shot, consumed by the next fixed step whatever the ground, so the script owns eligibility (a grounded check, coyote time, double jumps). `speed` must be finite and > 0. Requests before a step do not add up: the last one wins. |
| `bool teleportCharacter(flecs::entity, glm::vec3 position, float yaw)` | `physics.teleportCharacter(entity, position, yaw)` | World position of the origin and yaw in radians about +Y. Clears the velocity, the move intent, a pending jump, the inherited support velocity, the support and both interpolation endpoints, and writes the entity's local `Transform` at once (through the parent's inverse world matrix, keeping the local scale), so the frame draws it there. |
| `std::optional<CharacterState> character(flecs::entity)` | `physics.character(entity)` | The state the last step left, or nothing (`nil`). |

`CharacterState` holds `velocity` (world, m/s, support velocity included), `grounded` (standing on walkable ground: Jolt's `OnGround`), `sliding` (on ground too steep to stand on: `OnSteepGround`), and, while `grounded` or `sliding`, `groundNormal`, `support` (the entity whose body is stood on, which may since have gone) and `supportVelocity` (the support's velocity at the contact); they are zero and none in the air. Lua gets a table with the same field names, `support` an `Entity` or `nil`.

- **Invalid entities.** On an entity that is gone, disabled, has no `CharacterController` or whose controller is rejected, the commands return false and do nothing and `character` returns nothing; in Lua they return `false` and `nil`, and a destroyed `Entity` raises "the entity no longer exists" as it does everywhere.
- **When state exists.** Characters are created lazily like bodies: by the step, and on the spot by a command or `character` on an entity that should have one, so a script's `start` can teleport its character or set an intent before the first step. Until a step has moved it, `grounded` and `sliding` are false and the velocity is zero. Disabling the entity destroys the character; enabling it creates a new one where the entity is. Edit mode has none, and stopping play discards them with the snapshot.
- **Outside writes.** A `Transform` written by anyone but physics (a script, the inspector, a gizmo, an undo) is found by the step as it is for dynamic bodies and teleports the character there, with everything `teleportCharacter` clears. A written rotation is an outside write too: its yaw becomes the controller's, and a tilt rejects it as above.

### The step

- **Jolt.** Each character is a `JPH::CharacterVirtual` with an inner body: a kinematic capsule of 0.9 times the radius in the moving layer, with sleeping turned off on it and an entry in `m_bodyInfo` naming the controller's entity. The inner body is what queries, triggers and fast dynamic bodies see; the virtual character is never in the broad phase. Characters meet each other through those inner bodies: no `CharacterVsCharacterCollision` until two characters that push each other are needed.
- **Order inside `PhysicsStep`.** (1) Stale bodies and characters are destroyed and missing ones created, controllers validated. (2) Poses in: static bodies, kinematic `MoveKinematic`, outside writes to dynamic bodies and characters. (3) Characters update, in entity-id order, on the stepping thread: each consumes its commands, sets its velocity and calls `ExtendedUpdate`, against the world as the last step left it and with this step's kinematic velocities, so a platform's motion is the character's support velocity. Their pushes on dynamic bodies happen here, before the solver runs. (4) `PhysicsSystem::Update` on the job system. (5) Contact records become events. (6) Poses out: dynamic bodies and characters. The characters use the step's `TempAllocatorImpl` and body interface strictly before `Update` begins and after the previous one has returned with its jobs released ([ADR-0013](0013-job-system.md)), so nothing races the solver or the allocator. `ScriptFixedUpdate` then runs, so `fixedUpdate` reads the state of the step just taken and its commands reach the next one.
- **Velocity.** Before `ExtendedUpdate` the wrapper calls `UpdateGroundVelocity` and builds the velocity as the feasibility test's wrapper does. Standing on walkable ground with no jump pending, the vertical velocity is the support's and gravity adds nothing: no creep on a walkable slope, no throw when a lift stops. Otherwise gravity (the world's, `PhysicsDesc::gravity`, with no per-character scale) is added each step, so the character keeps and accumulates its vertical velocity in the air and on steep ground. While grounded, the support's horizontal velocity is inherited; it is kept unchanged in the air (no air drag) and replaced on landing; sliding on steep ground clears it. The horizontal velocity is the intent plus the inherited velocity; `ExtendedUpdate` itself removes the part that pushes up a steep slope. A consumed jump replaces the vertical velocity with the support's vertical velocity (when grounded) plus the jump speed, then adds the step's gravity, so a late coyote jump goes as high as one from the ground, and it inherits the support's horizontal velocity like any departure. `ExtendedUpdate` gets the world's gravity for pressing on its support, `stepHeight` as `mWalkStairsStepUp` and `groundSnap` as `mStickToFloorStepDown`, `mass` and `pushStrength` as `mMass` and `mMaxStrength`. Rotating supports are out of scope ([#499](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/499)).
- **Contacts.** A character's trigger events are its inner body's, through the existing listener and its per-pair counting ([physics.md](../physics.md#events)): `TriggerEnter` and `TriggerExit` with the controller's entity. Its solid contacts are its own, from a `CharacterContactListener` that records added and removed contacts with non-sensor bodies into the same records, on the stepping thread during step (3), so `ContactBegin` and `ContactEnd` reach scripts through the existing `onContactBegin` and `onContactEnd` hooks with the same ordering and cap; landing on a floor is a begin and a jump off it an end. The character listener's sensor contacts and the inner body's solid contacts are dropped, so neither event comes twice. The events of a step are sorted as today, whatever thread recorded them.
- **Queries.** `raycast` and `sphereCast` ([#507](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/507)) hit a character's inner body and report the controller's entity. `ignore` naming a controller's entity skips its inner body, which is all there is of the character in the broad phase, so a camera cast that ignores the player cannot hit it. `debugLines` draws the capsule in its own colour.

### The frame

A rendered frame in play mode runs, in order:

1. The application hands scripts the camera that was drawn last frame (what `camera.ray` unprojects through: what the pointer was over), as today.
2. `FixedUpdate`, zero to `maxFixedSteps` times: `PhysicsStep` with the characters inside it, then `ScriptFixedUpdate` (contact events, then `fixedUpdate`).
3. `Update`: `AnimationPlayback` (the clips advance and are posed, the events recorded), then `ScriptUpdate` (button and animation events, then `update`).
4. `PostUpdate`: `PhysicsInterpolate`, now for characters too (position between their last two steps by `fixedAlpha`, rotation constant between teleports), then a new `ScriptLateUpdate` calling `lateUpdate(self, dt)`, declared after `PhysicsInterpolate`. Scripting takes the physics world in its constructor, so its systems are always declared after physics' ones. `lateUpdate` sees the interpolated poses (`worldPosition()` reads them through the hierarchy), which is where a follow camera places itself.
5. `PreRender`: the previous world transforms, the transform system, the skin palettes, audio.
6. After `progress`, the application reads the drawn camera with `world::sceneCamera`, then builds the draw list. `Game::update` and `Editor::update` move their `sceneCamera` read after `progress` for the drawn view; the audio fallback listener follows the drawn camera.

- **Zero-step frames** (a frame shorter than what is left of a step): no physics, no `fixedUpdate`; the clips advance by the frame's delta, the interpolation moves on with the larger `fixedAlpha`, `update` and `lateUpdate` run. Commands wait for the next step.
- **Multi-step frames**: the intent applies to every step; a jump is consumed by the first; `fixedUpdate` runs after each step and may issue new commands for the next. The interpolation runs once, between the last two steps.
- **Time scale 0**: the frame pipeline runs with a zero delta and no steps; `update` and `lateUpdate` get `dt` 0 (a camera's smoothing must not divide by it), poses stay where they were drawn, a pending jump waits for the steps to resume.
- **Paused** (`setPaused`, the editor's pause): the edit pipelines run, so none of the simulation systems do, `lateUpdate` included; the transform system and the camera read still run, so the frame is the last one drawn, and resuming continues from the kept accumulator.
- **Locomotion weights** follow physics state, which changes only in fixed steps: a script that computes them in `fixedUpdate` from `physics.character` has them applied by the same frame's playback; computed in `update` they apply one frame later.

### Animation weights and synchronisation

- **Primary weight.** `Animator` gains `weight` (default 1, valid 0 to 1). The primary clip's blend weight is `weight` times the crossfade envelope, the private ramp that is 1 except while a crossfade raises it over `fade` seconds. A crossfade creates its outgoing layer at the effective weight it had (weight times envelope) and its time, falling to zero over `fade`; the outgoing layer is not synchronised and plays on at its own speed. Layers keep their own `weight`. A blend is the weight-normalised sum, per target, of the layers with a positive weight that have a channel for it, so a weight of exactly 0 contributes nothing: pure idle, walk and run are one weight at 1 and the rest at 0, and a mix is any weights that do not all vanish. Scripts keep the weights summing to 1 for clarity; normalisation makes it unnecessary. A target that no layer with a positive weight reaches keeps the pose it last had. With `weight` at its default, existing scenes animate exactly as before.
- **`AnimationLayer::sync`** (default false). A synchronised layer follows the primary's phase instead of its own time: after the primary advances, the layer's `time` is the primary's phase (`time / duration`) times the layer's duration, and its own `speed`, `playing` and `loop` are ignored while it is synchronised.
  - The primary paused, stopped at the end of a non-looping clip, played backwards, or looping: the phase does the same, so the layer holds, sits at its own end, runs backwards or wraps when the primary does.
  - The primary missing (no clip, or not loaded yet) or of zero duration: the layer holds its time and plays nothing new until the primary is back, then jumps to its phase.
  - A new primary clip: the layer follows the new clip's phase from the next frame. Assigning `clip` does not change `time`; a script that switches gaits and wants the phase kept sets `time` to the old phase times the new duration in the same `set`.
  - Events: a synchronised layer crosses the phase interval the primary crossed this frame, mapped onto its own duration, with the primary's direction and wrap, and fires them when its weight is at least 0.05 as any layer does. A primary whose `time` was assigned crosses nothing, so neither does the layer.
  - Synchronising phases does not align gaits: the clips must be authored, or trimmed at import, so their foot contacts fall at the same phases (left foot down at 0 and right at 0.5 in walk and run alike).
- **Speed-matched playback** (guidance for T9, [#496](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/496)). Each locomotion clip has a stride: the ground distance one cycle covers, measured from the clip (root or planted-foot travel over one loop) and kept by the script as a property. With the primary and the synchronised layers blending gaits at blend `t`, the feet do not slide when the cycles per second equal the ground speed over the blended stride: `primary.speed = primaryDuration * groundSpeed / mix(strideA, strideB, t)`, where `groundSpeed` is the horizontal length of `character.velocity - character.supportVelocity`, measured rather than intended, so walking into a wall slows the feet. Below a small speed the script blends towards idle by weight instead of slowing a walk towards zero, and it keeps the playback rate within a band the clips survive (about 0.7 to 1.4) by changing gait rather than stretching one. `rootMotion` stays off on a character rig: the controller is moved by intent, and a root motion write would be an outside write that teleports it; driving the controller from root motion is a later decision.

### Versioning

`CharacterController` is a new component, and `Animator.weight` and `AnimationLayer.sync` are new fields with defaults that keep today's behaviour, so the scene format stays at its version, existing scenes load unchanged, and cooked bundles carry scenes as CBOR documents with no bundle bump. `IPhysicsWorld` (a public header), the `physics` Lua table and the `lateUpdate` hook grow, which is a `feat` and a MINOR bump before 1.0.0 ([conventions.md](../conventions.md#versioning)); 1.0.0 freezes them with the rest.

## Consequences

- A character is a root entity with a `CharacterController` and a model child, driven by four calls, with its camera in `lateUpdate`; it rides translating platforms, climbs steps and stops at slopes with no game code for it.
- The wrapper owns the velocity rules (gravity only off walkable ground, support inheritance, jump replacement), so they are the engine's to test and keep, not Jolt's sample code to copy. T2 tests them through `IPhysicsWorld` in play mode, including zero-step and multi-step frames, outside writes, rejection and the contact events; the feasibility tests stay as the record of what Jolt itself does.
- Physics now steps characters on the main thread, one after another, before Jolt's parallel update. A handful of characters costs little; a crowd would want `CharacterVsCharacterCollision` and a different design.
- The drawn camera is one frame fresher in the player and the editor, for every scene, not only characters.
- `docs/physics.md`, `docs/world.md` and `docs/scripting.md` describe the component, the commands, the step order, the weights, `sync` and `lateUpdate` when T2, T4 and T6 land.
- Root motion on characters, rotating supports ([#499](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/499)), character-versus-character pushing and foot IK ([#500](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/500)) are later work.

## Alternatives considered

- **Jolt's `Character` (a rigid body with a capsule)**: simulated by the solver, so it pushes and is pushed for free, but it bounces on steps, slides on slopes and cannot step up or snap down without the same sweeps `CharacterVirtual` already has.
- **No inner body**: one representation fewer, but raycasts, sphere casts and triggers would not see the character at all, and every query would need a second path through the characters.
- **The character's own sensor contacts for triggers**: no inner body needed for them, but the inner body is needed for queries anyway, and it reports triggers through the existing listener and counting.
- **A full-vector `setCharacterVelocity`**: simpler to describe, but every script would integrate gravity and handle support itself, each a little differently, which is exactly what the feasibility test showed is easy to get wrong (slope creep, lift throws).
- **A grounded check inside `jumpCharacter`**: safer for the simplest script, but coyote time and double jumps would need a way around it; the state already says whether the character is grounded, and a consumed jump leaves the ground at once.
- **Jolt's sample velocity rule (keep inertia unless moving towards the ground, gravity always added)**: it throws a character up a few centimetres when a lift stops and makes it creep down walkable slopes.
- **Characters after `PhysicsSystem::Update`**: they would see platforms already moved and lag them by a step, and their pushes would wait a step to act.
- **`lateUpdate` in a new `LateUpdate` phase**: explicit, but a new public phase for one system; declaring it after the interpolation within `PostUpdate` is guaranteed by construction order.
- **A blend tree or state machine asset for locomotion**: the right tool for many gaits, and still possible later on top of weights and `sync`; three clips and a script do not need it.
