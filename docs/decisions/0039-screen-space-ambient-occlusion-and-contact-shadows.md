# ADR-0039: Screen-space ambient occlusion and sun contact shadows

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Amends:** [ADR-0008](0008-clustered-forward-rendering.md)
- **Date:** 2026-11-08

## Context

[#508](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/508) settles the inputs, lighting terms, controls and mobile budget before ambient occlusion ([#501](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/501)) and contact shadows ([#509](https://github.com/Pacheco95/sonnet/issues/509)) are built. The character course needs local grounding around feet, corners and small props, without baking a shadow into their materials or replacing the cascaded shadow maps.

The renderer is clustered forward. Its opaque and masked depth pre-pass writes reversed-Z `D32Sfloat` depth, plus `R16G16Sfloat` motion under TAA; it writes no normals. With occlusion culling, `depth late` completes that depth after the pyramid used for the second cull has already been built. Material occlusion currently multiplies diffuse and specular image-based lighting (IBL), or flat ambient without an environment, but no direct lighting. The renderer already keeps separate temporal state for the editor's Scene and Game views ([ADR-0021](0021-two-views-in-one-frame.md), [ADR-0024](0024-temporal-anti-aliasing-and-occlusion-culling.md)). Blended, transmissive and particle surfaces write neither pre-pass depth nor its motion.

ADR-0008 allows a thin normal/roughness G-buffer for screen-space effects. Adding one now would add a full-resolution attachment to every participating pre-pass, including its late draws. An octahedral normal in `R16G16Sfloat` alone is 7.9 MiB at 1920×1080 per view, before a roughness channel: one write and one read are about 16.6 MB per frame, or 1.0 GB/s at 60 frames/s, ignoring caches and compression. It also needs vertex normal interpolation, and material normal maps would need tangents and texture reads. Depth reconstruction trades those writes for neighbouring depth reads and ALU, but cannot recover normal-map detail or hidden surfaces. Dynamic rendering local read does not remove the neighbourhood reads these effects need; it is not a way to keep this work entirely on a tile.

The [S25 measurements](../reports/s25-m14.md) and [transmission measurements](../reports/transmission-mobile.md) show why a cold GPU timing alone cannot enable a phone effect: thermal throttling can erase the apparent margin. The [ADR-0019](0019-vulkan-1.3-devices-with-the-1.4-extensions.md) feature baseline remains sufficient; neither effect needs ray tracing, a new Vulkan feature or a vendor-specific path.

## Decision

**AO is half-resolution ground-truth ambient occlusion (GTAO), reconstructed from depth, spatially filtered and, under TAA, accumulated in its own scalar history using TAA's motion and jitter. Contact shadows are a separate full-resolution, short depth-buffer ray march towards the sun. Neither adds a normal attachment to the pre-pass.** These are implementation contracts for #501 and #509; accepting this ADR adds no rendering pass or saved field by itself.

### Inputs and pass ordering

- Both effects read this frame's **completed** opaque and masked depth, after `depth late` when occlusion culling is enabled, or after the ordinary pre-pass otherwise. They never read the early culling pyramid as a substitute for final depth. They work with occlusion culling off and introduce no dependency between #501 and #509.
- Reconstruct view-space positions using the inverse of the **jittered raster projection**, with the renderer's reversed-Z and negative-viewport convention. The existing unjittered sky inverse is not the right inverse for those depth samples. Perspective and orthographic views both work. Depth 0 is sky: visibility is 1, and it contributes no occluder.
- Reconstruct a geometric normal from depth neighbours, choosing the side of each axis that best continues the centre surface, using the next neighbour to detect a depth discontinuity. Cross those derivatives and orient the result towards the camera. Do not average positions across a silhouette; an invalid or degenerate reconstruction gives neutral visibility. A normal map continues to affect material lighting, not the screen-space horizon or ray bias.
- Declare the AO and contact passes between completed depth and forward shading; clustering may stay between them and forward. All sampled inputs, attachments and history imports go through the render graph. Full-screen graphics passes can write the existing `R8Unorm` and `R16G16B16A16Sfloat` colour formats; this decision needs no new storage-image format or bindless array. Forward samples the completed visibility masks, not its bound depth attachment.
- The passes, masks, sample sequence and AO history belong to each view. Two views of different sizes never share them. A disabled effect, zero strength/length, or a view without pre-pass draws declares none of that effect's passes. Contact shadows additionally require an enabled sun and `RendererSettings::shadows`. No sun or cascades are required for AO. An absent mask means visibility 1.

### Ambient occlusion

**Search.** At `ceil(width / 2) × ceil(height / 2)`, choose the closest non-sky depth sample in each full-resolution 2×2 footprint (the maximum in reversed-Z), and retain its position for the filters and motion lookup; never average foreground and background depths. Integrate cosine-weighted visibility from the two horizons of each azimuthal slice through that point's hemisphere, the GTAO method. Start with three evenly spaced slices and four depth samples in each direction per slice, 24 horizon samples per half-resolution pixel. The radius is in world metres, projected separately for perspective and orthographic cameras; samples outside it contribute nothing and the contribution falls smoothly to zero at the radius. Off-screen samples contribute no occlusion. The per-pixel rotation and sample offset are a deterministic function of raster jitter, varying through the eight TAA phases and staying fixed without jitter. An explicit `referenceJitter` selects the corresponding search pattern without accumulating history. There is no bent-normal output or separate specular-occlusion model in #501.

**Filter.** Use a separable five-tap bilateral filter at half resolution and a depth/normal-aware upsample into a full-resolution `R8Unorm` visibility mask. Both use the representative surface's depth and reconstructed normal, reject incompatible surfaces and renormalise surviving weights. An upsample with no compatible half-resolution sample is unoccluded. This deliberately loses subpixel occluders rather than putting a dark fringe on a background surface. At an odd image size the last footprint uses only its valid pixels. The full-resolution mask lets the forward pass take one sample without reading depth while it is an attachment.

**Temporal accumulation.** Under TAA, insert the AO temporal resolve between the horizon search and spatial filtering. It keeps two half-resolution `R16G16B16A16Sfloat` images per view, storing visibility, positive view depth and an octahedral world-space normal (7.9 MiB for the pair at 1080p). These guides are for history rejection, not a pre-pass normal target. Depth comparisons allow for the guide's half-float precision; non-finite or unrepresentable guides reject history.

- Use the motion of the selected full-resolution depth sample, including object and skinned motion, not the closest unrelated motion in a neighbourhood. Motion is current unjittered UV minus previous unjittered UV. In raster UV coordinates the history lookup is `currentUv - motion + previousJitterUv - currentJitterUv`, where jitter UV is the actual raster displacement, including the Y convention. TAA's HDR history is not an AO input: it contains lighting and cannot be reused as scalar visibility before forward shading.
- Reject off-screen history and mismatched depth or normals, comparing guides in a common coordinate frame. Clamp accepted history to the compatible current neighbourhood's visibility range, and give the current frame at least 1/8 of the weight. Disocclusions use the current estimate alone; moving geometry may reject more history rather than keeping another surface's occlusion.
- Reset on first use, resize, projection changes, `SceneView::resetHistory`, a skipped view frame, AO enable/disable or parameter/sample-count changes, and any frame without a TAA resolve (including debug views and `referenceJitter`). Retire unused history with the renderer's existing eight-frame retention rule and deferred resource destruction. None and FXAA use only the fixed search pattern and bilateral filter, without motion or AO history; `referenceJitter` applies the supplied projection jitter and corresponding search pattern but keeps no temporal state.

**Lighting.** Let `A` be the filtered screen-space visibility in [0, 1], `s` the camera's strength in [0, 1], and `M = lerp(1, occlusionTexture.r, material.occlusionStrength)` the existing material occlusion. The indirect multiplier is `M * lerp(1, A, s)`. Apply it to diffuse and specular IBL, or to flat ambient when there is no environment, once in `forward.slang`. It never multiplies sun or punctual direct light, emissive, the skybox or the final composed colour. It does not multiply ADR-0033's transmitted-light sample, which already contains the opaque scene's shaded light. Unlit surfaces receive no AO.

Only opaque and masked receivers in the pre-pass sample screen-space AO. Blended/transmissive surfaces and particles do not read the opaque surface's mask as their own; their existing material occlusion remains as today. Opaque surfaces seen through a transmission copy already include their AO once. Opaque or masked unlit geometry still contributes depth and can occlude a lit neighbour.

### Sun contact shadows

- Trace once per full-resolution pre-pass pixel towards `-SceneView::sun.direction`, in view space, for the light's finite world-space length. Start with 16 steps. Bias the origin along the reconstructed normal using its pixel footprint to avoid self-intersection, then project the ray samples with the same raster projection that produced depth. Compare positive linear view depths, not differences in nonlinear reversed-Z values. A sample is a hit only when it is behind a visible surface by more than the self-intersection tolerance and less than `contactShadowThickness`; a foreground silhouette is not an infinitely thick occluder.
- Visibility is 1 with no hit, tends to 0 for a nearby hit, and fades back to 1 over the last 20% of the ray length. Fade the effect at the screen boundary over four pixels; a ray leaving the screen or crossing the near plane contributes no further occlusion. Test a small box and a thin post against a plane to tune the bias and thickness without halos. A back-facing receiver needs no trace because its sun contribution is already zero.
- Write an `R8Unorm` visibility mask. TAA's subsequent HDR resolve smooths the shaded result, so there is no contact-shadow history and no dependency on the AO temporal resolve. Offset steps deterministically from raster jitter, as for AO, so an explicit `referenceJitter` reproduces the corresponding pattern. Without jitter, including debug views, use a fixed pattern. Half-resolution tracing is not the reduced mode: it loses the small contacts this effect is meant to restore. The reduced mode uses eight full-resolution steps.
- For an opaque or masked receiver, `sunVisibility = min(cascadeVisibility, contactVisibility)`. The sun's direct diffuse and specular light both use that visibility; taking the minimum avoids multiplying two estimates of the same partial shadow. Outside cascade coverage `cascadeVisibility` is 1, so a visible local occluder can still cast its short contact shadow. With all shadows disabled there is no trace and sun visibility stays 1.
- The setting belongs to each **directional light**, but only the first enabled directional light selected by `world::sceneLight` is the rendered sun, as today. Point and spot lights keep their existing `castsShadows` maps and get no contact-shadow fields or trace in #509. Blended/transmissive receivers use only their ordinary shadow maps; they and particles cannot cast screen-space contact shadows because they write no pre-pass depth. Off-screen and hidden occluders are also absent; the shadow maps remain necessary.

### Settings and saved controls

Renderer-wide controls bound cost; scene controls author the effect. A renderer toggle is an upper bound: a saved scene request cannot override a platform that has disabled the effect.

| Owner | New fields and defaults | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `RendererSettings` | `ambientOcclusion = true`, `contactShadows = true` | Permit the corresponding scene request; each can be disabled independently |
| `RendererSettings` | `ambientOcclusionSlices = 3`, `ambientOcclusionSteps = 4`, `contactShadowSteps = 16` | Search counts; AO steps are per side of a slice. Clamp slices to 1–4, AO steps to 1–8 and contact steps to 1–32 |
| `world::Camera`, carried through `renderer::Camera` into `SceneView` | `ambientOcclusion = false`, `ambientOcclusionRadius = 0.5`, `ambientOcclusionStrength = 1.0` | Per-view AO request, radius in metres, and strength |
| `world::DirectionalLight`, carried into `renderer::DirectionalLight` | `contactShadows = false`, `contactShadowLength = 0.2`, `contactShadowThickness = 0.02` | Per-sun request, maximum ray length and view-depth hit thickness in metres |

AO belongs to the camera, beside exposure and bloom, because it is a view-dependent approximation and must also work with flat ambient and no `Environment`. The environment retains its map and intensity; it gets no duplicate AO controls. For rendering, clamp AO radius to 0–2 m, strength to 0–1, contact length to 0–1 m and thickness to 0–length. Radius, strength, length or thickness zero makes its effect neutral. Non-finite inputs use the field's default. These are bounds on the values handed to the renderer, not silent edits to authored component data.

- Reflect the scene fields in `World` so the inspector, Lua, undo, scenes, prefabs and instance overrides see the same values. Extend `placedCamera`/`sceneCamera` and `sceneLight`, then the editor and `runtime::Game` handoff to `SceneView`. No shader or renderer code reaches up into `world`.
- The Game view and player use the first enabled scene camera, or the existing fallback with AO off. The editor Scene view previews that same camera's AO controls while retaining its own fly-camera pose and projection, so an inspector edit is visible in both views. Both views use the scene's selected sun. Cut each affected view's AO history when its source camera or effective AO controls change. A standalone renderer caller sets the `SceneView` values directly.
- The new scene fields are additive with defaults that preserve existing scenes: AO and contact shadows remain off until authored. Scene/prefab schema version **9 stays 9**, following ADR-0038's additive-field rule; no existing member changes meaning and no migration is required. Cooked scenes already carry reflected component JSON as CBOR, so the bundle layout/version stays unchanged too. Tests must load an old scene with the fields omitted and round-trip explicit values and prefab overrides through source and cooked loading.
- #501 and #509 each land their own fields, renderer controls, reflection, editor/player plumbing and tests together, before the [1.0.0 format freeze](../roadmap.md#100). Their capability changes are `feat` changes and bump MINOR before 1.0.0; this documentation-only ADR does not bump the engine version. Their character-course captures explicitly enable the relevant camera/light requests, and existing showcase content is not silently opted in.

### Mobile budget and measurement

The following are **acceptance budgets, not measured performance**. Desktop renderer defaults permit both effects at the standard counts. Android and iOS players initially disable both through `RendererSettings`, even if the scene requests them. Quality is chosen by the application, not inferred from Vulkan 1.3 versus 1.4; ADR-0019 gives the same rendering capabilities, not a speed guarantee.

| Candidate | AO resolution and horizon samples | Contact resolution and steps |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Half on each axis; 3 slices × 4 steps × 2 sides | Full; 16 |
| Reduced | Half on each axis; 2 slices × 3 steps × 2 sides | Full; 8 |
| Off | No AO work | No contact work |

Measure AO alone, contact shadows alone and both together against both off. An effect can ship independently. The added **total GPU frame time**, including forward sampling, filtering, histories, barriers and attachment stores/loads, must be at most **1.5 ms for AO**, **0.5 ms for contact shadows**, and **2 ms combined**. Require these limits for the median and p95 of complete GPU-frame samples in both cold and settled sustained windows. The sustained p95 frame interval must also remain under **33 ms**, and the peak thermal status and time to throttling must be no worse than the matched off run. No valid GPU samples means the cost gate is unproven; a partial sum of available pass timestamps or a vsync interval is not a substitute.

The implementation reports record:

1. The commit, Release build, device/driver and Vulkan form, renderer settings, render/window extents, present mode, frame count, thermal state and GPU clocks where available. Use the RTX 4090 at 1920×1080 and the Galaxy S25 Ultra/Adreno 830 on ADR-0019's 1.3 extension path at both 1920×1080 and its native landscape extent. An Android result does not enable iOS: measure an iOS/MoltenVK device separately before changing its default.
2. The character course with the same stationary camera and a deterministic traversal, plus the existing showcase stress scene, with TAA and shadow maps on. Keep assets, sun, exposure, simulation, resolution and every unrelated setting identical across each on/off pair. Separate asset and pipeline warm-up from measured frames and settle TAA for at least 32 frames.
3. At least three paired cold windows of three seconds after warm-up, alternating on/off order, and paired sustained runs of at least 600 seconds of **wall-clock time**, extended until clocks and thermal state settle. Deterministic `--play` seconds count fixed simulation steps; they do not by themselves establish a ten-minute thermal run. Report median/p95 GPU totals and frame intervals, per-pass times, sample availability and transient/persistent image memory. Re-establish the off baseline at the same starting thermal conditions for each pair.
4. On the S25, read `adb shell dumpsys thermalservice` before each cold start: start at once with skin at or below 31 °C and thermal status 0; above that, wait and re-read, never assume a fixed cooldown is enough. Record temperature/status and, where available, clocks during the sustained run, not just at its end.

Use the highest candidate that passes the visual checks and every performance/thermal gate for the measured platform. Try Reduced if Standard fails; keep an effect off if Reduced fails or remains unmeasured. Do not add automatic runtime quality oscillation or change the scene's saved requests. If both pass alone but fail together, keep AO as the first candidate, then test it with reduced contact shadows; if that still fails, leave contact shadows off. Publish the actual timings and choice in `docs/rendering.md` and a linked report under `docs/reports/` for #501 and #509. Neither issue can claim its phone measurements from this ADR.

### Implementation acceptance

- **Graph/null-device checks:** completed-depth ordering with occlusion culling on and off; independent toggles and neutral settings declare no unnecessary passes; no normal attachment; correct odd/1-pixel extents; AO history invalidation; two views at different sizes with no shared masks or history. All image lifetimes and history imports obey existing graph and deferred-destruction rules.
- **AO GPU checks on Lavapipe:** a corner and a box on a plane occlude locally, an isolated open plane stays unoccluded, and the effect falls away outside its world-space radius. Exercise flat ambient, diffuse IBL and specular IBL separately; direct-only and emissive/unlit fixtures remain unchanged. A material occlusion value multiplies AO exactly once. Depth edges, alpha masks, orthographic cameras, skinned motion and transparency/transmission exclusions have explicit fixtures.
- **Contact GPU checks on Lavapipe:** a small box on a plane gains a contact its coarse shadow map misses; an isolated plane has no self-shadow; a thin post/depth discontinuity has no dark halo. Cover sun direction and length, thickness, per-light opt-out, global shadows off, absent sun, and AO off. Punctual and indirect light are unchanged. The combined sun factor follows the minimum rule, including outside cascade coverage.
- **Temporal checks for both:** after 32 settling frames, measure a whole eight-frame jitter cycle, then move the camera and occluder and remove it. Use fixed numeric tolerances in the tests for visibility drift and trail recovery, with a spatial reference of the same held pose; a vacated region must recover within four frames. Test None/FXAA with the fixed pattern, cuts, resize, skipped frames, source-camera changes and two simultaneous views. `referenceJitter` must leave histories unused and permit repeatable spatial references.
- **Integration and evidence:** old/default-off scenes retain their output; authored requests survive scene/prefab save/load, undo, play/stop and cooking, and the editor Game view and source/cooked player agree at the same pose and settings. Update any intentionally changed Lavapipe goldens with the cause stated. Each implementation supplies character-course on/off captures, RTX 4090 and phone timings, and its report linked from `docs/rendering.md` and its issue. #509 can do this with AO disabled; #501 can do it with contact shadows disabled.

## Consequences

- Opaque and masked surfaces gain local indirect occlusion and short sun contacts without growing the geometry pre-pass or material format. The two effects have independent passes, settings and implementation issues.
- Extra sampled-depth traffic, masks, filtering and AO history are real costs even without a G-buffer. The editor pays them per view; on a tile-based GPU, passes may force depth stores and reloads. The mobile gates measure those costs as part of the whole frame.
- Reconstructed normals lose normal-map detail, and half-resolution AO can miss thin geometry. Screen-space effects lose occluders outside the view or behind the closest surface and fade near the view boundary; they cannot replace material occlusion, shadow maps or geometry-aware lighting. Scalar AO on specular IBL remains the existing approximation, not directional specular occlusion.
- The saved controls are settled before 1.0.0, without a schema migration or an appearance change to old scenes. #501 and #509 must update the relevant world, editor, player and rendering documentation when their implementations land.

## Alternatives considered

- **A normal/roughness pre-pass attachment:** gives stable interpolated or mapped normals and could serve other effects, but adds full-resolution bandwidth and material work that these two effects do not require. No roughness input is needed for the chosen scalar AO. A later effect can justify a thin G-buffer in a new ADR.
- **Full-resolution GTAO:** preserves finer contacts but roughly quadruples horizon searches; half resolution with edge-aware reconstruction is the initial cost/quality choice. Contact shadows retain full resolution specifically for small sun contacts.
- **Simple hemisphere SSAO or HBAO:** viable depth-based choices, but GTAO's cosine-weighted horizon integral is the selected ambient-visibility estimate; extra material knobs or a second AO technique would complicate validation without meeting another requirement.
- **Only the final TAA resolve as the AO filter:** avoids scalar history, but half-resolution horizon noise first modulates the lit signal and HDR clipping cannot distinguish that noise from changing illumination. Separate visibility history reuses the existing motion and reset contract while keeping the lighting composition correct.
- **AO multiplied into the final colour:** cheap as a post effect but incorrectly darkens direct light, emissive and the sky. Applying the mask to transparent foreground surfaces likewise shadows them with another surface's visibility.
- **Contact shadows for every clustered light:** multiplies the ray-march cost by light count and needs a selection budget and punctual controls. #509 addresses the single sun; point/spot extensions require a later decision.
- **Ray-traced AO or contact shadows:** includes hidden/off-screen geometry but would require new acceleration structures and hardware features outside the current baseline.
