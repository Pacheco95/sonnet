# gui

The game UI: menus, a heads-up display, a pause screen as entities in the scene, with components that `gui` registers, lays out and hit-tests ([ADR-0026](decisions/0026-game-ui.md)). Depends on `world` publicly, so `core`, `platform`, `rhi`, `renderer` and `assets` come with it. `scripting` links it to read its events, as it links `physics`. It is not `ui`, which stays Dear ImGui for the editor and which the player never links.

This is M15 in parts: the components, layout, input and events; the drawing of images and rectangles; the editor's view of a canvas and the sample menu; and fonts and text, which completes the UI.

| Header | Contents |
|---|---|
| `Components.h` | `GuiCanvas`, `GuiRect`, `GuiImage`, `GuiText`, `GuiButton`, `GuiLayout`, `GuiFit`, their enums, and `registerComponents` |
| `Layout.h` | `layoutGui`: the rectangle of every element, in draw order; `GuiElement`, `PixelRect`, `TextMeasure`, `findElement` |
| `GuiSystem.h` | `GuiSystem`: the layout of a frame, the events offered to it before the game, the `GuiEvent`s it records, and the quads and the pass that draw it |

## Components

The components are plain structs registered with the world's reflection ([world.md](world.md#components)), so the inspector edits them, scenes save them (the scene stays at version 3: components are added, none changed) and scripts read and write them like the core ones. `GuiSystem` registers them; `registerComponents` does it alone, for tools that load scenes without running a UI (the editor), and does nothing the second time. Every length is in reference pixels, which the canvas scales to the target.

- `GuiCanvas` is the root of a tree: `referenceSize` (1920 × 1080), `match`, `safeArea` and `order`.
- `GuiRect` is an element's place inside its parent's rectangle: `anchorMin` and `anchorMax` (0 to 1 over the parent), `pivot`, `offsetMin` and `offsetMax`. The element runs from the parent's point at `anchorMin` plus `offsetMin` to its point at `anchorMax` plus `offsetMax`: a fixed size at a corner, a stretched panel and a centred button are the same fields. An element's parent is its parent entity, which holds a `GuiRect` or a `GuiCanvas`; the `Transform` every entity has is not read. An entity without a `GuiRect` under the tree is not an element, and nothing under it is either.
- `GuiImage` has a `texture` (an identity), a `tint` (sRGB, with alpha) and `slice`, the nine-slice borders in texture pixels. `blocksInput` makes it take pointer events without a button.
- `GuiText` has a `font` (an identity), `text`, `size`, `color`, `horizontal` and `vertical` alignment and `wrap`. `font` is a Font asset ([assets.md](assets.md#fonts)); without one the text draws nothing, and the inspector says so.
- `GuiButton` has `interactable` and four tints, normal, hovered, pressed and disabled, for the `GuiImage` on the same entity. A scene saved before `hovered` existed loads its default. It carries no script: the `Scripts` on the same entity hear its events.
- `GuiLayout` on an element sizes and places its children in a row or a column, in sibling order.
- `GuiFit` sizes its element to its text plus `padding` (left, top, right, bottom) ([Layout](#layout)).

An entity with the `Disabled` tag is hidden and takes no input, and so is everything under it: the layout and the hit test apply the tag to the subtree themselves, which the tag alone does not do for the children.

## Layout

`layoutGui(world, targetSize, safeArea, measure)` is a plain function from the entity tree, the target's size and a text measurer to a rectangle per element, in target pixels, run once a frame by `GuiSystem::update` after the world has updated. Nothing is cached. The result is in draw order: canvases by `order` and then entity, and under each canvas its tree depth first in the order of `World::children`, so what comes later is in front.

- **Scale.** The target against the canvas's `referenceSize`, by width at `match` 0 and by height at 1; in between the two logarithms are blended, so a target twice as wide and 1.5 times as tall scales by √3 at 0.5. A canvas scales from the whole target, not from its safe area, so a notch does not change the size of text.
- **Safe area.** A canvas with `safeArea` takes the part of the target inside the platform's ([platform.md](platform.md#window)); an empty one is no inset.
- **Anchors.** As `GuiRect` says, with the offsets multiplied by the scale. `pivot` is reported in the result, in target pixels, and moves nothing yet: the corners define the rectangle, and there is no rotation or scale for a pivot to turn about.
- **Layout.** Under a parent with a `GuiLayout`, the children's anchors are ignored and each takes its size, `offsetMax - offsetMin`. They run along the axis from the padding (`left, top, right, bottom`) with `spacing` between; `alignment` puts the block along the axis and each child across it at the start, the centre or the end of the padding's inside. Hidden children take no room.
- **Fit.** An element with a `GuiFit` takes the size of the largest `GuiText` on it and on its visible child elements, each measured as one unwrapped block (its widest line by `ascent - descent` per line), plus the padding, times the scale. The size replaces the `GuiRect`'s: under a `GuiLayout` it is the child's size, and otherwise the anchored rectangle keeps its pivot's point and grows or shrinks about it. Text is measured by `measure`, a `TextMeasure` that `GuiSystem` gives from the fonts' metrics alone (advances and kerning through `layoutText`, no atlas); a layout without one, an element without text, and one whose text has a font that is not loaded keep the `GuiRect`'s size. Fonts load when their text is first drawn, so a fitted element keeps its authored size for the first frame and takes its text's from the next. Text in a fitted element does not wrap, since the element is as wide as its longest line; fitting to images and to nested elements, and minimum and maximum sizes, are not done.

## Input

`GuiSystem::event` is offered each `platform::Event` before the game, and says whether it consumed it. A press (a mouse button or a finger) that lands on an interactable `GuiButton` or on a `GuiImage` with `blocksInput`, front to back over the elements, is consumed and its pointer is held by that element until it is released; so are the motion and the release of a held pointer. Everything else is the game's. A label or a panel without those is not in the way. `runtime::Game::event` calls it first, so a consumed pointer never reaches `InputState` or `input.touches()`.

Positions are window coordinates and are mapped to the target by the ratio of the sizes given to `update`. SDL's mouse synthesised from a finger arrives as well as the finger ([platform.md](platform.md#events)); both land on the same button, which counts them as one press until the last is released. Only the left mouse button and fingers make events; another mouse button over a button is consumed and says nothing. Losing the window's focus lets go of every held pointer, without a click.

**Hover.** The mouse's last position, from its motion, presses and releases, is kept, and `update` hit-tests it after the layout, front to back like a press, so the hover follows a button that moves under a still mouse; `GuiSystem::hovered(entity)` answers for the front-most element there, which a `blocksInput` image in front of a button takes instead. A finger is no cursor: the mouse SDL synthesises from one (`fromTouch`) hovers over nothing, so a tap on a phone leaves no button highlighted. The mouse leaving the window (`MouseLeftWindow`) and a focus loss forget the position. Hovering consumes nothing: the game still gets the motion.

## Events

`GuiSystem` records a `GuiEvent` (kind, entity, pointer) for a button: `Press` when a pointer goes down on it, `Release` when the last one holding it goes up, and `Click` after that release when it was over the button. `scripting` delivers them at the start of each `update` frame as `onPress(self)`, `onRelease(self)` and `onClick(self)` on the instances of the button's entity, in slot order ([scripting.md](scripting.md#events)). The application calls `endFrame` after the world's frame; `Game` does.

## Drawing

`GuiSystem::addPass(renderer, assets, graph, target)` builds the frame's quads and declares the renderer's overlay pass ([rendering.md](rendering.md#frame-structure)) over `target`. `runtime::Game::render` calls it after the scene passes and before the screenshot, into the same target the player copies to the swapchain and the capture reads, so a capture shows what the player sees. A game with no canvas, or none that draws, declares no pass and renders byte for byte as before.

- **What is drawn.** Each element with a `GuiImage`, in the layout's draw order (a parent, then its children in sibling order; later is in front). Without a texture it is a plain rectangle of its tint. Its `GuiText` follows, a quad for each visible glyph ([Text](#text)).
- **Colour.** Authored in sRGB with alpha and blended as it is, in the display-encoded space the target is already in. The texture's colour is multiplied by the tint, so the UI image has to be an sRGB colour texture (the import default).
- **Buttons.** A `GuiButton` on the same entity multiplies the image's tint by its `disabled` (not `interactable`), `pressed` (while a pointer holds it), `hovered` (under the mouse) or `normal` tint, the first that holds.
- **Nine-slice.** With a non-zero `slice` (left, top, right, bottom in texels) and a texture the image is nine quads: the corners keep the border's size times the canvas scale, the edges and the centre stretch. Borders wider than the rectangle shrink together to fit, and a band with no room is left out.
- **Loading.** An image whose texture is still being imported draws nothing, rather than a white rectangle that would flash, and is asked for again on the next frame.
- **Not clipped.** Elements are not clipped to their parents or to the safe area.

## Text

`GuiText` draws with the `font` it names, from `text`, at `size` reference pixels times the canvas scale, in `color`. It is drawn in the same pass as images, after the image of the same element, so a label on a button needs no ordering of its own.

- **Fonts.** `FontCache` loads a font from the asset database on first use and again when its file is read again, parsing it once with `stbtt_InitFont` from a copy of the bytes, so a hot reload cannot free what a worker is still reading. A nil `font`, an identity that is not a font and bytes that do not parse draw nothing; there is no placeholder and no built-in default font, which would be a file in every player.
- **Glyphs.** A glyph is rasterised on first use with `stbtt_GetGlyphSDF` at 48 pixels to the em with a 6-pixel border, one size for every size drawn, as a signed distance field with 128 on the outline. It is packed into an R8 atlas page of 1024² by shelves, one atlas for each font, found by glyph index, so every character a font lacks shares its notdef. A glyph seen for the first time in a frame is in the atlas that frame. The 48 was measured: the sample font at 32, 48 and 64 against a 128-pixel reference at 12, 24 and 96 pixels differs from it by a mean of 0.12, 0.06 and 0.03 of 255 at 12 pixels and 0.20, 0.15 and 0.09 at 96, and the three are not told apart by eye, so the smallest atlas that holds is kept.
- **Preload.** The ranges in the font's sidecar are rasterised when the font loads, on `core::JobSystem` through the asset database's pool ([ADR-0013](decisions/0013-job-system.md)), and move into the atlas on the main thread the first frame after the job has finished. Glyphs drawn before that are rasterised on the spot, and the job's copy of them is dropped.
- **Upload.** An image is filled once, so a page that gained glyphs is created and uploaded again whole (1 MB) and the old one goes through the device's deferred destruction, at most once a frame for each page: a page that changes again in that frame keeps its glyphs for the next one, and its quads leave out only a glyph that is on no uploaded page yet. A page that was never uploaded has no texture to draw from, and its quads are left out rather than drawn from the white default.
- **Layout.** `layoutText` is pure and runs without a GPU. Text runs left to right, each glyph at the pen, which then moves by its advance and the font's kerning for the pair (`stbtt_GetGlyphKernAdvance`: this version of stb reads a font's `kern` table, or its GPOS pair adjustments when it has them, which is more than the ADR promised); there is no shaping, no right-to-left text and no input method. `\n` ends a line. With `wrap`, a line ends before a word that would pass the element's right edge, a word longer than the element breaks between characters (a character is always placed, however narrow the line), and the spaces at a break are dropped. Lines follow `horizontal` inside the element and the block of lines follows `vertical`; a line is `ascent - descent + lineGap` tall, as the font says. Invalid UTF-8 becomes U+FFFD.
- **Size.** A `GuiText` does not measure itself for a parent `GuiLayout`: a child's size is its `GuiRect`'s, as it is for an image, unless a `GuiFit` sizes the element to its text ([Layout](#layout)).
- **Shader.** A glyph quad has the glyph kind (`OverlayKind::Glyph`), and `overlay.slang` reads coverage from the red channel of the atlas, `saturate((d - 0.5) / fwidth(d) + 0.5)`, so the edge is one pixel wide at any scale, and uses `color` as it is, without the encode an image's texel gets ([rendering.md](rendering.md#frame-structure)). The pass is still one draw, however images and glyphs interleave.
- **Scripts.** Nothing is added to Lua: `self.entity:set("GuiText", { text = "Score 12" })` changes a label, and the next frame lays it out again. A string member reads and writes as a Lua string through reflection, which it did not before.

## In the editor

The editor owns a `GuiSystem` like the player's `Game` does ([editor.md](editor.md#game-view)). It lays the canvases out against the Game panel's image every frame, in edit mode as in play mode, so a layout is seen while it is made; draws them into the Game view's target after the scene's passes; and, in play mode with the panel focused, offers the panel's pointer events to the UI before the game. Pausing, stopping, losing the focus or closing the panel calls `GuiSystem::releaseAll`, which lets go of every held pointer without a click; then and whenever the mouse is off the panel's image, the editor offers the UI a `MouseLeftWindow`, so nothing stays hovered, and in edit mode, which takes no input, nothing hovers at all. A selected element is outlined over the image. The viewport (the editor's own camera) shows no UI.

## The sample

`apps/samples/basic/scenes/menu.scene.json` is the menu: a `Dim` image that blocks input, a nine-sliced `Menu` panel with a column of three buttons (Play, Options, and a Quit), and an `OptionsPanel` popup that starts `Disabled`. `scripts/quit_button.lua` ends the game with `game.quit()` when Quit is clicked and hides the button where `game.canQuit()` is false ([scripting.md](scripting.md#game)). `scripts/toggle_button.lua` shows or hides its `target` entity when its button is clicked, which is how Options opens the popup and Close shuts it. `scripts/scene_button.lua` loads the scene in its `scene` property with `game.loadScene`: Play starts `scenes/playground.scene.json`. Run it with `sonnet_player apps/samples/basic --scene scenes/menu.scene.json`.

The playground is the game behind it, with a `HUD` canvas: a `Score` label that `scripts/score_label.lua` keeps up with the total the pickups add to `score.lua`, a `Pause` button in the top-right corner, and a `PauseMenu` that starts `Disabled`: a dim layer that blocks input over a nine-sliced panel with Resume, Main menu and Quit in a column. `scripts/pause_button.lua` on Pause and on Resume, both pointed at the `PauseMenu` by its `menu` property, sets the time scale to 0 and shows the menu, or back to 1 and hides it ([scripting.md](scripting.md#game)); Main menu is a `scene_button.lua` back to the menu scene, which starts at real time again, and Quit is the menu's `quit_button.lua`. The dim layer covers the Pause button, so only Resume ends a pause.

`scenes/hud_stress.scene.json` is the HUD budget of M15 as a scene: a canvas whose `scripts/hud_stress.lua` builds two hundred elements in a grid of twenty by ten tiles, a third of them labels counting frames, so their glyphs are laid out again every frame, and the rest nine-sliced panels and plain rectangles. Its capture's `overlay` pass is the measurement ([player.md](player.md#capture-runs)). The canvas scales by width, so the whole grid is on screen in portrait too. On an RTX 4090 in Release at 1920×1080 it is 0.008 ms of GPU time, and on the Galaxy S25 Ultra at 1080×1920 0.085 ms with the GPU busy under the stress scene ([roadmap](roadmap.md#m15-game-ui-and-the-game-module-hook)).

## Tests

`gui_tests` run headless, without a GPU: anchors, offsets and pivots against hand-computed rectangles, row and column layouts with spacing, padding and alignment, the canvas scale for width, height and between, a safe-area inset, canvas order, a disabled subtree hidden and input-less, hit testing over overlapping canvases and siblings, capture of a pointer until its release, one click for a press and release inside and none for a release outside, a finger and its synthesised mouse as one press, a consumed touch absent from `InputState`, the window-to-target mapping, the hover (under the mouse, following a moved button, stopped by a `blocksInput` image, and gone after the mouse leaves, a focus loss and a finger's synthesised mouse), the scene round trip of every component and the default of a button saved without `hovered`, the quads of images (rectangle, tint, draw order, the button's state tints with pressed over hovered and disabled over both, a disabled subtree, a texture still loading), the nine-slice rectangles and texture coordinates against hand-computed values, and that the pass is declared only when something draws. `runtime_tests` draws a box through `Game` and finds it in the screenshot; `renderer_tests` reads a quad back from the top-left on Lavapipe, `runtime_tests` clicks the sample menu's buttons through `Game` (Options opens the popup, Close hides it, Quit asks the game to quit, the dim layer does nothing, and no click reaches the game's input), plays the sample through it (Play loads the playground, Pause stops the game's time and shows the pause menu, the covered Pause button does nothing, Resume goes on, Main menu returns to the title) and compares a capture of the menu with `tests/golden/menu.png` on Lavapipe, and `editor_tests` lays the UI out against the Game panel in edit mode. `scripting_tests` runs a Lua `onPress`, `onRelease` and `onClick`, and reads and writes a string member.

Text has its own tests. `gui_tests` read a bundled test font of five glyphs with round metrics (`modules/gui/tests/data`, with its attribution) against hand-computed advances, bearings, kerning and SDF bitmap, lay text out in all nine alignments, wrap it at spaces and inside a long word, break at `\n`, fall back to the notdef and survive empty and malformed text, pack an atlas without overlap, open a second page when one is full, upload a changed page once per frame, collect the preload from the job system, parse a font again when its file changes, draw the quads of a label in the place the glyph metrics give, and fit a button to its label: its width and height plus padding about its pivot and at twice the scale, the largest of its texts with its own counted and a disabled one not, in a column, and at its authored size without a measurer, without text, and until its font has loaded. `renderer_tests` reads an R8 distance field drawn at two scales back on Lavapipe (an intermediate value on the edge of each, the colour not encoded) and checks that glyph and image quads stay one draw. `assets_tests` import and cook a font into a version 5 bundle and read it back byte for byte, with its ranges, and refuse a version 4 bundle. `runtime_tests` compare the menu, now with its labels, with its golden, and run a script that changes a label's text and find the new glyphs in the frame after, and play the HUD stress scene and find its two hundred elements, sixty-seven of them labels, drawn in the overlay pass without a warning.
